# Video 2 — The derivative as local sensitivity

The derivative is the slope of a function at one exact input. We write f'(x).

The formal definition uses a limit: f'(x) = lim as h approaches zero of [f(x+h)-f(x)]/h. You do not need to fear the limit. It means: measure the slope over a tiny interval, then imagine that interval shrinking toward zero.

For f(x)=x², the derivative is 2x. At x=3 the local slope is 6. A small positive move in x produces an approximately six-times-as-large positive change in f.

This is sensitivity: how responsive is the output to a small parameter perturbation right now?
